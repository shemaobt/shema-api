# A rehearsal is credited to the scene its invitation named

## Context

The room marks a scene rehearsed when the team's own word says a rehearsal is done and the
Guide's previous line was the invitation that asked for it. Which scene it marks was read
from the **Scene pointer** at the moment the report came back — one turn, at least, after
the invitation was spoken.

While the offer to the classifier was narrowed to one scene the pointer barely moved, and
reading it late was indistinguishable from reading it early. ADR 0034 widened the offer to
every bead short of `engaged`, and with it a name said in the first scene began closing the
same-named beads in the scenes ahead. Measured in the round of 23 September: the pointer
jumped from the first scene to the third between an invitation and its answer, the report
was credited to a scene the team had not rehearsed, and the scripted Ruth run needed one
more invitation-and-report pair to close.

The same jump reaches the Guide, whose ledger block heads that scene `CURRENT SCENE`. The
line computes the first scene whose beads are not all closed; read as *where the team is*,
it can hand the Guide a scene to open while the one before it was never opened.

## Considered Options

**Narrowing the offer again.** Rejected: that is the trap of 22 September — a bead left
merely mentioned holds its scene, the scene holds the offer, and no later bead can ever
move.

**Waiting for Marcia's word before touching anything.** Rejected as the whole answer: the
question of whether one mention should close a being in every scene it appears in is hers,
and it has gone into the letter; but during the pilot a rehearsal miscredited and a scene
opened out of order are harms we can stop without her.

**Naming the line the way her renderer does** — "the scene the ledger last saw the team
in". Rejected: hers is set from outside and means what it says; ours is computed, and
lending it her sentence would trade one false reading for another.

## Decision

**A rehearsal is credited to the scene its invitation named, recorded on the turn the
invitation is spoken and read back when the report arrives.** The room already reads its
own last utterance to recognise a report; it recognises the invitation the same way, one
turn earlier, and keeps the scene with the session's state rather than deriving it twice
from a ledger that has moved on.

**The ledger's scene line says what it computes** — the first scene whose beads are not
all closed — and nothing in the block instructs. It stays information, as the doctrine
has it.

## Consequences

The credit stops depending on how fast beads close. A scene the team actually rehearsed is
marked even if the necklace filled ahead of them in the meantime, and the release gate and
the send-off, which read those marks, stop asking for a rehearsal the team already gave.

The Guide can still be told about a scene further on than the team, because the ledger can
close a scene the team only half worked. It is no longer told that is where the team *is*,
and it never was told to go there.

What remains open is hers: whether one mention of a being should close that being in every
scene it appears in. If she says it should not, the jump itself goes and this credit rule
stays correct anyway — it is right for the same reason under either answer.
