# A rehearsal is credited to the first scene still owed one, recorded on the invite turn

## Context

[ADR 0035](0035-a-rehearsal-is-credited-to-the-scene-its-invitation-named.md) moved the
credit of a rehearsal from the turn the report arrives on to the turn the invitation is
spoken on, and said the scene to record there is the one the invitation named — read from
the **Scene pointer** on that turn.

The pointer cannot say it. Coverage settles only after the turn has run, on the text seam
and on the voiced route alike, and the Guide invites the next scene on the very turn the
team told the one before: in the scripted Ruth run it tells and invites scene N+1 on the
turn the team told N. Before the settle the pointer still says N; after it, with every
bead offered (ADR 0034), it may already have jumped past N+1. Measured in the round of
23 September: recording the pre-settle pointer produced the invitations S1, S1, S3, S2,
and the run never reached `done`.

## Considered Options

**The pointer before the settle.** Measured wrong: it names the scene the team just told,
one behind the scene the Guide is inviting.

**The pointer after the settle.** The answer is written before coverage settles, so the
turn would have to be reordered to read it — and the jump ADR 0034 describes lands it
past the scene invited whenever a name said early closed beads ahead.

## Decision

**On the turn the Guide invites a rehearsal, the room records the first scene of the
passage still owed one — the first scene not yet among the practised scenes — and credits
that scene when the team reports the rehearsal done.** The pointer is never read for the
credit. The rest of ADR 0035 stands: the credit is recognised on the invite turn and read
back on the report, the ledger's scene line says what it computes, and the report gate is
unchanged.

## Consequences

The credit no longer depends on when coverage settles or on how fast beads close; it is
right under either of Marcia's answers to the one-mention question. The suite is green and
the Ruth run credits the first, second, third and fourth scenes in turn and reaches `done`.

The rule leans on the Guide inviting scenes in order and never re-inviting a scene already
practised, which the prompt requires. Where the Guide breaks it, the credit goes astray:
re-inviting a practised scene credits the next one owed, skipping a scene credits the one
skipped.
